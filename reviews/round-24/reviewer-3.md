## REQUIRED

**R1 — `ln h` is misdescribed as an eigenvalue *ratio* (§pore-fabric/"Transverse isotropy of the pore fabric", and the caption of `fig:fe-fabric-probe`).**
The section defines `H = h^{-2} m⊗m + h(I − m⊗m)` `(eq:fabric-transverse-h)`, whose eigenvalues are `(h^{-2}, h, h)` — the text says so itself ("unimodular eigenvalues `h^{-2}, h, h`"). The logarithm of that tensor's eigenvalue ratio is therefore `−3 ln h`, not `ln h`; `ln h` is the logarithm of a single eigenvalue (the transverse one, `h`). The body text nevertheless reads "the reported shape scalar is the unimodular fabric eigenvalue ratio `ln h`", and the `fig:fe-fabric-probe` caption asserts an exact identity, "this is exactly the logarithm of the unimodular fabric eigenvalue ratio of `(eq:fabric-transverse-h)`". The recorded artifact confirms the factor-of-three gap: for the coupled 45° probe, `H_eigenvalues = (0.9992864, 1.0003570, 1.0003570)` with `h = e^{ln h} = 1.0003570`, so `ln h = 3.57×10⁻⁴` while the log eigenvalue ratio is `1.07×10⁻³`. As written, a reader cannot reconcile the statement with the paper's own equation, in the one figure that carries the new scalar. Fix (wording only, no equation changes): call `ln h` "the logarithm of the unimodular fabric eigenvalue `h = e^{ln h}`" — or state explicitly which ratio is intended and give its value in terms of `ln h`. Apply in both places.

## Optional notes

- **O1 — basis `e₁,…,e₆` declared but only four members defined.** §fabric-biot introduces "the labels `e₁,…,e₆` denote this fabric-adapted orthonormal basis of symmetric tensors" and defines only `e₁` (volumetric), `e₂` (axial), `e₃` and `e₆` (the coupled in-plane pair); `e₄`, `e₅` (the `m`-shear modes) never appear, so the announced six-member basis cannot be reconstructed from the paper. Compounding this, the labels `e₃` and `e₆` coincide numerically with the Mandel component indices 3 (=33) and 6 (=12) of `(eq:example-mineral-stiffness)`. The text pre-empts the Mandel reading ("not the Mandel component indices"), which is the right instinct, but the chosen labels keep the collision live. Consider defining all six members or renaming the in-plane pair.
- **O2 — forward use of `m`.** `\mathbf m` first appears in §fabric-biot (inside `e₂` and the in-plane pair) but is only defined later, in §fabric-transverse ("a unit material axis `m`"). A one-clause definition at first use would remove the gap.
- **O3 — `\mathbf p₁, \mathbf p₂` against pressure `p` and first Piola stress `\mathbf P`.** The in-plane basis vectors differ from the pressure scalar only by boldface, in a section that is pressure-heavy. Used twice only; consider renaming.
- **O4 — two objects in the `e` family.** `\mathbf e₁…\mathbf e₆` (symmetric basis tensors, §pore-fabric) and `\mathbf e_t` (unit tangent, `(eq:experiment-plane-directions)`) share the accent; the subscript `t` distinguishes them but they are introduced in different sections with different meanings.
- **O5 — shared lowercase-`k` family.** `k` (permeability, §fe-fluid-closure) and `k_v, k_a, k_c` (the distention-stiffness 2×2 block, §fabric-biot) use the same letter for unrelated stiffness/transport quantities; likewise `G` (drained shear modulus, §fe-reference-problems) vs `\mathbf G` (distention tensor) is distinguished only by the declared bold convention.
- **O6 — "their maps are reported".** §fe-fabric ("Scope of these results") says the partial-drainage runs' "mass-balance and platen-equality diagnostics and their maps are reported", but no map of those runs appears in the manuscript; the maps are published only in the companion-site figure list (`figures/fe_map_partial_0.png`, `figures/fe_map_anisotropic_30.png` in `site/evidence.json`). Naming the location would let a reader find them.
- **O7 — minor cross-reference slip.** §fabric-biot: "The minimization in `(eq:fabric-equivalent-energy)` is therefore over `E_dis ∈ range 𝔻`" — the minimization is posed in `(eq:fabric-equilibrium)`; the energy equation is its objective, not the minimization.
- **O8 — four overbar meanings.** The overbar denotes (i) the mineral state reached by removing distention, (ii) an intrinsic per-phase density, (iii) a stress representation in the mixture frame, and (iv) a prescribed boundary datum. All four are enumerated in §kinematics, which is what makes this readable at all, but the load is heavy; a distinct mark for case (iv) (e.g. on the boundary flux only) would help.
