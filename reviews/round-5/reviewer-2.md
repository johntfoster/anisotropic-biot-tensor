# Reviewer 2: physical construction and source fidelity

**Recommendation: ACCEPT.** The manuscript supplies a physically interpretable, energy-consistent constitutive construction for its stated spherical-distention mechanism. I find no required correction to the phase stress balance, work normalizations, anisotropic extension, stiffness restriction, or reported limits. This recommendation concerns the physical derivation and its fidelity to the authorized source; it is not an independent determination of literature novelty or of experimental applicability.

## Principal findings

1. **The phase average and the work normalization are consistent.** At main.tex:135–162, intrinsic mineral Cauchy stress is weighted by current solid fraction; converting the result to Kirchhoff stress correctly uses J phi_s = phi_s0 bar J. Consequently the mineral Kirchhoff term carries phi_s0, while the pressure term carries phi_s0 bar J. The mixture energy and mineral energy are normalized by their respective reference volumes at main.tex:170 and main.tex:217. There is no missing current mineral Jacobian or extra volume-fraction factor. This agrees with the companion's phase average and trace conversion at paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:436–475.

2. **The equivalent energy follows from the full spatial balance.** Main.tex:189–233 changes independent variables from (F, bar J) to (a, bar F). Substitution cancels the pressure work and leaves phi_s0 times the complete mineral work at fixed a. Integrating that work gives W_A(a) + phi_s0 bar W_s(bar F). In particular, the integration does not infer shear stress from a mean-stress law: the full mineral energy is independently prescribed. The companion's scalar work cancellation, integration, and equivalent volumetric energy at paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:477–530 are recovered for spherical mineral variations. The two purely volumetric energy terms at sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:98–112 coincide with that elastic specialization. The new volume–shape term is the cross term of the prescribed quadratic mineral energy, with the correct reference-volume weighting.

3. **The anisotropic extension is conditional and justified within its conditions.** Spherical distention makes skeleton and mineral normalized shapes identical. Prescribing the full mineral Hooke law then supplies a stronger constitutive assumption than the companion's mean-stress specification. The manuscript states that distinction at main.tex:230–233. It uses the material logarithmic strain and its conjugate stress at sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:24–47, rather than silently identifying that stress with spatial Kirchhoff stress. The matrix-logarithm derivative in sections/logarithmic_derivative.tex:9–52 has the correct factors, continuous repeated-eigenvalue limit, and trace property. It preserves stress components when stress and strain have different principal directions.

4. **The stiffness restriction is necessary, not an optional calibration.** The spherical drained path determines the scalar distention energy at sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:49–81. Eliminating mineral volume at zero pressure gives the rank-one stiffness reduction at lines 150–163; inversion gives the compliance difference at lines 165–178. Its action is spherical strain alone. Thus an arbitrary independent anisotropic drained stiffness cannot be recovered with this mechanism. The manuscript makes this limitation clear both locally and at main.tex:384–393. The fact that isotropic drained shear stiffness must equal phi_s0 times mineral shear stiffness, explicitly stated at sections/limits.tex:52–54, is a substantial physical restriction and is correctly retained. The definitions of K and K_s also correctly distinguish spherical-strain coefficients from hydrostatic-stress bulk moduli for an anisotropic mineral.

5. **The pressure derivative and the limits are correct.** Differentiating the mineral equation at fixed F gives the scalar denominator and the shape contribution in the explicit Biot tensor. The pressure transformation retains the chain rule: the total stress pressure tangent is -B, whereas finite pressure changes require the integral in main.tex:350–359. The reference stress and solid storage identities in sections/limits.tex:5–30 follow from the same stiffness restriction and scalar equilibrium derivative. The isotropic residual at lines 32–54 is identical to the companion's elastic residual at paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:553–562. The finite unjacketed construction at sections/limits.tex:56–76 is also valid: a = 1 makes distention stress vanish, and spherical logarithmic stress maps to spherical spatial stress even when mineral logarithmic strain is anisotropic. The phase sum then gives total stress -p I and unchanged solid fraction.

6. **Admissibility is appropriately separated from algebraic solvability.** The positive-pressure mineral root is unique, and the stated scalar derivative condition gives local mineral-volume stability on a negative-pressure branch (sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:137–148). The manuscript separately requires positive phase volumes and explicitly does not claim stability against all finite deformations. Positive quadratic logarithmic energy is not a proof of global ellipticity; the manuscript avoids that inference.

## Independent numerical evidence

The reconstruction script passed for 20 positive mineral stiffnesses and rejected 20 incompatible stiffness pairs. Maximum reported errors included 6.66e-15 for the complete drained Hooke law, 1.39e-16 for the compliance restriction, 6.10e-10 for work equivalence, 5.08e-14 for the unjacketed state, and 4.00e-12 for reference storage.

The tensor script passed 273 states across 13 materials. It computes intrinsic stress from true mineral deformation and separately differentiates the mixture pressure potential. The maximum full spatial phase-stress versus energy-stress discrepancy was 8.65e-10; the pressure tangent discrepancy was 1.94e-9; the mineral-volume-gradient discrepancy was 7.29e-11. Its maximum stress–strain commutator norm was 2.61, so the tests genuinely exercise different principal directions. Rotation and symmetry checks also passed. Agreement of the trace was checked separately and was not used as a substitute for these tensor comparisons.

## Optional improvements

- At main.tex:135–138 or main.tex:217–233, explicitly call the mineral constitutive field a representative phase response. A sentence explaining that the prescribed phase law and spherical kinematics constitute a model assumption would further distinguish the construction from a pore-scale homogenization result. The existing introduction and conclusion already limit the claim sufficiently for acceptance.
- At sections/experiments.tex:69–88, give the largest full stress and pressure-tangent errors directly in the manuscript, or a compact table with the tolerances. The scripts already contain this evidence; exposing two numbers would make the verification easier to assess without running them.

The contribution is strongest as a restricted constitutive derivation that identifies which anisotropic stiffness pairs can coexist with spherical distention. Its limitations are sufficiently explicit to support that interpretation at JMPS review standards.
