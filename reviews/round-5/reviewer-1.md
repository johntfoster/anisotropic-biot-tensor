## Independent mathematical assessment

1. **Phase weighting, work, and integrability are consistent.** `main.tex:158–245` uses the correct conversion from current mineral volume to reference mixture volume: `J phi_s = phi_s0 barJ`. Thus the mineral Kirchhoff stress carries the reference factor `phi_s0`, while the intrinsic Cauchy stress carries the current factor `phi_s`. The change of variables from `(F, barJ)` to `(barF, a)` preserves all deformation components. Pressure cancels from the mineral virtual work, including its shear part. At fixed `a`, arbitrary mineral variations then integrate to `W_s = W_A(a) + phi_s0 barW_s(barF)`. This integration depends substantively on the prescribed mineral energy being independent of distention; that assumption is stated at `main.tex:217–220`.

   There is also a direct converse check. Set `q = ln a` temporarily for this review. Differentiating the reconstructed energy at fixed mineral volume gives the spherical contribution `W_A,q I` plus `phi_s0 dev(barTau_s)`. Pressure equilibrium gives `W_A,q = phi_s0 tr(barTau_s)/3 + phi_s0 p barJ`. Substitution returns the complete equation `tau' = phi_s0(barTau_s + p barJ I)`. Thus the energy preserves the full phase balance, rather than only its trace. No coaxiality is needed.

2. **The drained reconstruction and restriction are necessary and sufficient in the stated class.** `sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:49–81` first calibrates the spherical-strain response. The distinction between the contraction-defined `K_s` and hydrostatic-stress bulk compliance is correct and essential for anisotropy. With `r = 1 - K/(phi_s0 K_s)` used only as shorthand here, the distention energy is `K(ln a)^2/(2r)`. Eliminating mineral volume at zero pressure is a scalar quadratic minimization in logarithmic variables. Its Schur complement is exactly the stiffness in `sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:159–163`.

   More explicitly, let `v = C_s:I`. The reduced stiffness is `phi_s0 C_s - phi_s0 r (v tensor v)/(9K_s)`. Its contraction with `I` on both sides returns `9K`, so the use of `K` in the construction is self-consistent. The rank-one inverse formula gives exactly `r I tensor I/(9K)` as the excess compliance. This proves the equivalence of the stiffness and compliance conditions. Equality of quadratic drained energies for every symmetric logarithmic strain requires equality of their Hessians; hence the condition is necessary as well as sufficient. Positive `C_s`, `K`, and `r` give positive drained stiffness. The text properly restricts this result to its construction; it does not claim arbitrary independent mineral and skeleton stiffness tensors are admissible.

3. **The mineral equation and its branch condition follow from the correct held-fixed derivative.** Differentiation at fixed `F` leaves `J` and `dev(epsilon)` fixed, while changing mineral volume and distention. The factor of `barJ` in pressure work and the volume–shape cross term are both retained in `sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:115–135`. The scalar equation's derivative with respect to `ln barJ` is `K_s + r p barJ`. Its monotonicity proves the stated unique positive root for nonnegative pressure. At a stationary point, the mineral-volume Hessian of the pressure-transformed potential has the sign of this same expression. The distinction between a local mineral-volume minimum, global stability, and positive phase volumes at `sections/stress_reconstruction.tex:137–148` is correct. Positive logarithmic elastic coefficients alone would not prove finite-deformation strong ellipticity; the paper does not assert that they do.

4. **Finite and noncoaxial logarithmic derivatives are handled correctly.** The appendix's divided-difference derivative, continuous repeated-eigenvalue limit, self-adjointness, and trace identity are correct (`sections/logarithmic_derivative.tex:5–52`). Under scalar scaling of `F`, the spatial map `T -> F Dlog(F^T F)[T] F^T` is unchanged. The skeleton and mineral therefore use the same spatial transformation of a logarithmic conjugate stress, although their logarithmic strains differ spherically. This also validates the converse phase-balance check above for general anisotropic stress.

   In `main.tex:287–329`, the derivative of `I:C_s:dev(epsilon)` is `F Dlog(C)[dev(C_s:I)]`. There is no missing factor of two: the one-half in `epsilon = log(C)/2` cancels the two symmetric contributions from varying `F^T F`. Inserting this derivative into the implicit mineral equation produces the displayed Biot tensor. Its symmetry and spatial rotation covariance follow from the same map.

5. **The stress split and measurable pressure tangent agree.** The pressure transformation in `main.tex:252–285` correctly cancels the implicit mineral-volume derivative in the single-prime stress. Mixed differentiation then gives `partial sigma/partial p at fixed F = -B` (`main.tex`, equations `pressure-envelope` and `cauchy-pressure-tangent`). The paper also correctly distinguishes this instantaneous pressure tangent from multiplication of a drained stress increment by a pressure-independent coefficient: its integrated-pressure relation is necessary when `B` varies with pressure.

6. **Reference storage and limits are consistent.** Substituting the derived drained stiffness proves `B_0 = I - C^d:C_s^{-1}:I` in `sections/limits.tex:5–15`, with the tensor contraction order as printed. Contracting the same rank-one reduction with `C_s^{-1}:I` twice gives the printed solid storage `phi_s0 r/K_s` (`sections/limits.tex:16–30`). There is no illicit replacement of the anisotropic hydrostatic compliance by `1/K_s`; the cancellations follow from the restricted stiffness pair. Fluid compressibility is appropriately separate.

   For isotropic mineral stiffness, the volume–shape term vanishes, and the scalar mineral equation and spherical Biot tensor follow (`sections/limits.tex:32–54`). The additional isotropic shear restriction is explicitly retained. On the unjacketed path (`sections/limits.tex:56–76`), `C_s:epsilon = -pJ I` transforms to mineral Cauchy stress `-pI` even when strain is anisotropic. Taking the trace of that logarithmic equation and substituting `a=1` verifies the mineral equation. The constant solid fraction and total hydrostatic stress then follow exactly. These are finite-state checks, not merely reference linearizations.

## Numerical evidence and its limits

`examples/verify_reconstruction.py` passed for 20 materials, rejected 20 incompatible stiffness pairs, and reported maximum drained Hooke error about `6.7e-15`, work-equivalence error about `6.1e-10`, and unjacketed error about `5.1e-14`. `examples/verify_tensor.py` passed 273 states; maximum phase-energy error was about `8.7e-10` and pressure-tangent error about `1.9e-9`. The nonzero stress–strain commutator confirms that the sampled states exercise noncoaxial behavior. The code compares the constituent phase-stress route against finite differences of the pressure potential, which is a meaningful implementation check. These tests corroborate the analytical arguments above; they do not prove global finite-strain stability or experimental adequacy.
