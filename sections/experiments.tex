\section{Constitutive examples and engineering consequences}
\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{A controlled mineral-anisotropy comparison}

The direct mineral contribution can be isolated without changing the mixed
coupling tensor. Let \(\bm v=\I/\sqrt3\) and
\(\bm T=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,2)/\sqrt6\). These are orthonormal
volumetric and axial deviatoric directions. Consider
\begin{equation}
 \Hh=3\phi_{s0}K_s\bm v\otimes\bm v
 +2\phi_{s0}\mu_s\Pdev+\beta\bm T\otimes\bm T
 +c(\bm v\otimes\bm T+\bm T\otimes\bm v).
 \label{eq:controlled-mineral-family}
\end{equation}
For \(\beta>0\), this mineral is already anisotropic at \(c=0\), but
\(\Pdev:\Hh:\I=\bm0\). With the isotropic coupling
\(\D=d_v\Pvol\), changing \(c\) at fixed \(K_s,\mu_s,\beta,d_v\)
gives, in the restricted daughter model,
\begin{equation}
 \bm B_0=\left(1+\frac{d_v}{3K_s}\right)\I
 +\frac{c}{\sqrt3K_s}\bm T,
 \qquad h=\phi_{s0}K_s.
 \label{eq:controlled-mineral-biot}
\end{equation}
The mineral tensor is positive definite if \(\mu_s,K_s>0\),
\(2\phi_{s0}\mu_s+\beta>0\), and
\(c^2<3\phi_{s0}K_s(2\phi_{s0}\mu_s+\beta)\).
In an arbitrary stress unit \(K_*\), take
\(\phi_{s0}=0.75\), \(K_s=20K_*\), \(\mu_s=8K_*\),
\(\beta=6K_*\), and \(d_v=-18K_*\).
Increasing \(c\) from zero to \(6K_*\) changes the Biot principal values
from \((0.700,0.700,0.700)\) to \((0.629,0.629,0.841)\), while the
mineral remains positive definite. Its mean Biot coefficient is unchanged.
Volumetric mineral deformation produces an axial deviatoric stress only in
the second case. This is the physical meaning of the projected mineral block.

The drained stiffness can be kept fixed by selecting \(\C^c\) through
\eqref{eq:drained-stiffness} and then \(\C\) through
\eqref{eq:constrained-skeleton-tangent}; the constrained energy is stable for
any positive-definite selected \(\C^d\). This comparison isolates an
energy contribution, not a homogenization of a specified pore geometry. In
particular, it does not impose the unjacketed compatibility conditions of
\cref{sec:compatibility}.

\subsection{A finite-deformation model with compatible reference tangents}

For the pressure and layer examples, choose the following complete energy,
which is objective because its logarithmic strain and material coefficients
are expressed in the reference frame:
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm H}(\bm\varepsilon,q)
 =\frac12\bm\varepsilon:\C^d:\bm\varepsilon
 +\frac h2(q-\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)^2,
 \qquad \bm r=-h^{-1}\bm g.
 \label{eq:example-hencky-energy}
\end{equation}
Here \(\C^d\) denotes the constant zero-pressure tangent in logarithmic
strain coordinates. At the reference state it is also the infinitesimal
drained tangent; away from that state it is not the spatial stress--strain
tangent. Let the material symmetry axis be \(\bm e_3\). Specify
\(\phi_{s0}=0.75\), an isotropic intrinsic bulk modulus \(K_s=20K_*\),
and the drained Mandel matrix in the order \((11,22,33,23,13,12)\):
\begin{equation}
 [\C^d]_{\mathrm M}=K_*
 \begin{pmatrix}
 12&4&3&0&0&0\\4&12&3&0&0&0\\3&3&8&0&0&0\\
 0&0&0&6&0&0\\0&0&0&0&6&0\\0&0&0&0&0&8
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:example-drained-matrix}
\end{equation}
The Mandel shear coordinates are \(\sqrt2\varepsilon_{23}\),
\(\sqrt2\varepsilon_{13}\), and \(\sqrt2\varepsilon_{12}\).
Using \eqref{eq:mixed-tangent-compatibility} and
\eqref{eq:compatible-mineral-tangent} gives
\begin{equation}
 h=24.39759036K_*,\qquad
 \bm r=\operatorname{diag}(19,19,14)/45,\qquad
 \bm B_0=\operatorname{diag}(41,41,46)/60.
 \label{eq:example-parameters}
\end{equation}
Thus both unjacketed reference conditions are satisfied. In particular,
\(h\ne\phi_{s0}K_s=15K_*\). These numbers define a synthetic material;
\(K_*\) is a common stress scale, not a measured shale modulus.

The scalar mineral equilibrium and its tangent are
\begin{align}
 h(q-\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)+\phi_{s0}p\exp(q)&=0,
 \label{eq:example-equilibrium}\\
 h_p&=h+\phi_{s0}p\exp(q)>0\quad(p\geq0).
 \label{eq:example-positive-tangent}
\end{align}
For every prescribed \(\bm\varepsilon\) and \(p\geq0\), the residual
is strictly increasing from a negative to a positive value as \(q\) varies
over the real line, so the equilibrium is unique. Porosity admissibility must
still be checked. Eliminating \(q\) from \(W_{\mathrm H}+\phi_{s0}p\exp(q)\)
gives a logarithmic-strain Hessian
\(\C^d+h\,z(1+z)^{-1}\bm r\otimes\bm r\), where
\(z=\phi_{s0}p\exp(q)/h\geq0\); it is positive definite. As noted in
\cref{sec:finite-hencky}, this does not assert global finite-strain stability.

For stretches coaxial with the material axes,
\(\F=\operatorname{diag}(\exp\varepsilon_1,
\exp\varepsilon_2,\exp\varepsilon_3)\), the stress and tangent simplify to
\begin{align}
 \sigma_i&=J^{-1}(\C^d:\bm\varepsilon)_{ii}
 -p\left(1-\frac{\phi_{s0}\exp(q)}Jr_i\right),
 \label{eq:example-coaxial-stress}\\
 B_i&=1-\frac{\phi_{s0}\exp(q)}{J(1+z)}r_i.
 \label{eq:example-coaxial-biot}
\end{align}
The factor \(1+z\) makes the distinction between the tangent and the
finite-pressure stress change explicit. The first term in
\eqref{eq:example-coaxial-stress} is the exact zero-pressure stress at the
same deformation, since \(q=\bm r:\bm\varepsilon\) when \(p=0\).

\subsection{Compatible anisotropic intrinsic solids}

The mineral mechanism can also be examined while enforcing both reference
unjacketed conditions. Retain the drained tensor
\eqref{eq:example-drained-matrix} and take the mixture-normalized mineral
family \eqref{eq:controlled-mineral-family}, now allowing the coupling to
change as compatibility requires. With \(\Hh=\phi_{s0}\bar{\mathbb H}\),
set \(\bm a=\phi_{s0}\Hh^{-1}:\I\), determine \(h\) and \(\bm g\)
from \eqref{eq:compatible-mineral-tangent} and
\eqref{eq:mixed-tangent-compatibility}, and define
\begin{align}
 \bm t&=3\bm g-\Pdev:\Hh:\I,
 \label{eq:compatible-daughter-target}\\
 \D&=\tfrac13\bm t\otimes\I-\tfrac12\Pdev:\Hh:\Pdev,
 \label{eq:compatible-daughter-coupling}\\
 \C&=\C^d+h^{-1}\bm g\otimes\bm g,
 \qquad \kappa=h-\tfrac19\I:\Hh:\I.
 \label{eq:compatible-daughter-reconstruction}
\end{align}
Since \(\D:\I=\bm t\) and
\(\D:\Pdev+\Pdev:\D^T=-\Pdev:\Hh:\Pdev\), this construction
returns \(\C^c=\C\), the prescribed \(\C^d\), and the compatible
\(\bm g,h\). It exhibits the daughter coefficients rather than treating
them as implicit fitting parameters. The energy is positive definite on its
constrained state space when \(\C^d\) and \(h\) are positive.

For the mineral family, inversion in its
volumetric--axial-deviatoric subspace gives
\begin{equation}
 \bm a=\frac{\phi_{s0}\sqrt3}
 {3\phi_{s0}K_s-c^2/(2\phi_{s0}\mu_s+\beta)}
 \left(\bm v-\frac{c}{2\phi_{s0}\mu_s+\beta}\bm T\right),
 \label{eq:compatible-mineral-compliance}
\end{equation}
\Cref{tab:compatible-minerals} gives three compatible materials. Every
mineral tensor and every reduced energy is positive definite. Using their
\(\bm r=-\bm g/h\) in \eqref{eq:example-hencky-energy} also gives
admissible states at \(s=-0.2,0,0.2\) and
\(p/K_s=0,0.01,0.1\), with sampled porosity above 0.13. Here \(K_s=20K_*\)
is only the common normalization for those pressure samples; the anisotropic
intrinsic hydrostatic compliance is the tensor \(\bm a\).

At \(c=0\), the deviatoric anisotropy has no influence on \(\bm a\).
At \(c=6K_*\), changing only \(\beta\) changes the inverse mineral
response and therefore the compatible Biot tensor, even though
\(\Pdev:\Hh:\I=\sqrt3c\bm T\) is unchanged. There is no contradiction
with the fixed-coupling result: compatibility now changes \(\D:\I\)
as well. The identifiable prediction under homogeneous-solid assumptions is
\(\bm B_0=\I-\C^d:\bm a\), not a separately observable attribution to
\(\Hh\) or \(\D\). Thus purely deviatoric mineral changes are invisible
when volumetric and deviatoric response are decoupled, but can influence
pressure coupling through compliance when those blocks interact.

\subsection{Pressure changes after prescribed predeformation}

Hold the specimen at \(\F=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\exp s)\), for
\(s=-0.2,0,0.2\), and increase pressure from zero to \(0.1K_s\).
Compare the full stress with two approximations along these coaxial paths:
\begin{equation}
 \bm\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}(\F,p)=\bm\sigma(\F,0)-p\bm B_0,
 \qquad
 \bm\sigma_{\mathrm{scalar}}(\F,p)
 =\bm\sigma(\F,0)-p b_0\I,
 \qquad b_0=\tfrac13\tr\bm B_0.
 \label{eq:comparison-stresses}
\end{equation}
Both retain the full model's entire zero-pressure response. The tensor
approximation also reproduces every reference pressure-tangent component;
the scalar approximation reproduces only its mean. These are specified
stress approximations on coaxial loading paths, not alternative globally
hyperelastic finite-deformation models.

\subsection{Uniform pressurization of a laterally confined layer}

Consider a homogeneous layer of reference thickness \(L_0\), with its lower
surface fixed vertically, zero lateral strain, and zero total vertical
traction on its upper surface. Pressure is prescribed uniformly after fluid
equilibration, gravity is neglected, and the symmetry axis is normal to the
layer. The exact homogeneous boundary-value solution has
\(\F=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\exp s)\), with
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_3(s,p)=0,\qquad
 \frac{u_3(L_0)}{L_0}=\exp(s)-1,
 \qquad \sigma_1=\sigma_2
 \quad\text{(lateral reaction stress)}.
 \label{eq:confined-layer-problem}
\end{equation}
The full model solves \eqref{eq:example-equilibrium} and the vertical
traction condition together. Each approximation solves the same traction
condition with its own stress from \eqref{eq:comparison-stresses}. Thus
the compared displacements satisfy the same boundary conditions; the full
solution is not inserted into the approximate stress laws to infer extension.
This is an equilibrium boundary-value example, not a consolidation transient.
Its small-pressure limits provide independent checks:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\dd s}{\dd p}\right|_0=\frac{B_{03}}{C^d_{3333}},
 \qquad
 \left.\frac{\dd\sigma_1}{\dd p}\right|_0
 =\frac{C^d_{1133}}{C^d_{3333}}B_{03}-B_{01}.
 \label{eq:layer-initial-slopes}
\end{equation}

At \(p/K_s=0.1\), extension is 30.6\% in the full model, 27.7\% with

regime. For a stiff mineral, pressures approaching \(0.1K_s\) may lie well
outside the reversible range of a real rock. The larger loads expose the
constitutive mechanism; their suitability for a particular engineering
material must be established experimentally.

\subsection{Finite-pressure information not fixed by reference calibration}

The finite-pressure numbers above are conditional on the selected energy.
To quantify that constitutive freedom, replace the logarithmic mineral-volume
penalty in \eqref{eq:example-hencky-energy} by a penalty in the volume ratio:
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm V}(\bm\varepsilon,q)
 =\frac12\bm\varepsilon:\C^d:\bm\varepsilon
 +\frac h2\left[\exp(q)-\exp(\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)\right]^2.
 \label{eq:volume-penalty-energy}
\end{equation}
The two energies have identical reference Hessians in
\((\bm\varepsilon,q)\), and at every zero-pressure deformation both
minimize at \(q=\bm r:\bm\varepsilon\) with the same condensed energy.
Consequently they share both reference unjacketed conditions, reference
storage, and the full zero-pressure stress response. Yet the volume-penalty
model gives
\begin{align}
 \bar J_{\mathrm V}&=\exp(\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)-\frac{\phi_{s0}p}{h},
 \label{eq:volume-penalty-equilibrium}\\
 \Phi_{\mathrm V}&=J-\phi_{s0}\exp(\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)
                    +\frac{\phi_{s0}^2}{h}p,
 \label{eq:volume-penalty-pore-volume}\\
 B_{{\mathrm V},i}&=1-\frac{\phi_{s0}}J
                   \exp(\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)r_i
 \quad\text{on coaxial paths}.
 \label{eq:volume-penalty-biot}
\end{align}
This tensor depends on deformation, but not pressure. It satisfies
\eqref{eq:biot-integrability}, in contrast to freezing the spatial reference
tensor. Its mineral tangent is \(h_p=h\bar J_{\mathrm V}^2>0\) on the
positive-volume branch. The model is used only where
\(0<\phi_{s0}\bar J_{\mathrm V}/J<1\); it is not extended past mineral
volume collapse.

Solving the same confined-layer boundary conditions with
\eqref{eq:volume-penalty-energy} gives extension 29.88\% and lateral stress
\(-0.04336K_s\) at \(p/K_s=0.1\), compared with 30.60\% and
\(-0.04459K_s\) for the logarithmic penalty. These correspond to 2.4\%
and 2.8\% differences relative to the logarithmic model. The differences
are smaller than the frozen-tensor errors in this example, but they remain
undetermined by all of the shared calibration data. At \(p/K_s=0.01\),
the extension difference falls to 0.19\%. These two continuations are an
explicit demonstration of nonuniqueness, not upper and lower bounds on all
admissible energies. Their complete pressure sweeps are generated by the
same reproduction script.

A finite-pressure experiment at fixed deformation can distinguish them.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:example-coaxial-biot} at zero pressure gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial B_i}{\partial p}\right|_{\F,p=0}
 =\frac{2\phi_{s0}^2r_i}{Jh}
   \exp(2\bm r:\bm\varepsilon)
 \quad\text{for }W_{\mathrm H},
 \qquad
 \left.\frac{\partial B_{{\mathrm V},i}}{\partial p}\right|_{\F}=0.
 \label{eq:pressure-curvature-discriminator}
\end{equation}
The stress curvature is the negative of this derivative, by
\eqref{eq:cauchy-pressure-tangent}. Measuring successive equilibrated
pressure slopes at a fixed deformation, together with intrinsic-density or
pore-volume changes, supplies information absent from reference elastic
moduli and storage. Experimental resolution and the reversible pressure
range determine whether that curvature is distinguishable in a real material.
The present results establish the constitutive distinction and a test for it;
they do not supply that experimental evidence.

\subsection{Reproducibility and verification}

The script \texttt{examples/finite\_pressure.py} uses bracketed scalar
equilibrium solves and generates all plotted data and table entries under
\texttt{build/examples/}. The manuscript build executes it automatically.
The tensor-check script \texttt{examples/verify\_tensor.py} evaluates
\(\log\bm U\) and its spectral derivative for general, including
noncoaxial, deformation gradients. It compares pressure and deformation
finite differences against the Biot tensor and compares the gradient of
\(W'-pJ\) against the total Piola stress. It also checks superposed-rotation
covariance, tensor symmetry, the daughter-energy reduction, the
coaxial formulas, analytical initial layer slopes, and both reference
unjacketed conditions. It shares the scalar root routine and material
constants with the generator, but implements the general tensor derivatives
separately. The compatible anisotropic daughter reconstructions and the second
hyperelastic continuation are checked as well.

At 28 test states, the largest absolute discrepancy in the pressure-tangent
check is below \(8\times10^{-8}\). Across 103 propagation directions per
state, the smallest sampled acoustic-tensor eigenvalue is above
\(2.10K_*\). The alternative volume-penalty model also has a sampled acoustic minimum
above \(2.10K_*\), and its pressure/potential finite-difference errors are
below \(3\times10^{-10}\). These are local numerical checks, not a proof
of global ellipticity. All tabulated pressure and layer states have \(h_p>0\) and
porosity strictly between zero and one. The source specifies the deterministic
sampling seed and writes the full diagnostics and software versions for
reproducibility.
